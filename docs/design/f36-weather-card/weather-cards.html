Weather cards

Prototypes for F36: the card Enigma draws above a weather answer, filled from the Open-Meteo forecast, with the model's short description streaming in under it. Every figure on a card comes from the forecast data (WeatherForecast), never from the model's text. Use the controls to compare the two sky styles and the two frames at the panel's and the window's widths. Replay shows the card arriving with the data.

In a reply

One card per place. A single day shows now (or that day's high and low) and the next hours; several days show one column each.

Every sky

The background follows the condition and the time of day: day, sunrise and sunset, and night. Storm lightning is a soft flash every 6 to 16 seconds.

Questions for the plan

  • Flat or soft sky?Flat matches UI-Style.md: solid colour, outlined shapes, hills like the character's line. Soft uses gradients, blur and glow, which the style guide rules out everywhere else.
  • Split or full sky?Split puts the figures on the app's own surface, so they follow Paper and Ink and stay readable over rain. Full sky is more striking, but the hourly row then sits over falling rain and snow.
  • Hours for a future day.The forecast holds the next 24 hours only, so tomorrow's strip (3-hourly here) needs the hourly request extended to 48 hours, or the strip dropped for future days.
  • Sunrise and sunset sky.Here it's the 45 minutes either side of the day's sunrise and sunset, worked out from the daily times beside is_day.
  • Snow amounts.The Oslo card shows precipitation in mm. Snow in cm needs snowfall_sum added to the daily request.
  • Lightning.Kept dim and rare (at most 14% brighter, about half a second) so it stays calm and is safe for people sensitive to flashing.
